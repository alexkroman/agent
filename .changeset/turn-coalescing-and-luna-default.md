Coalesce the caller's queued turns into one reply, and put the default gateway model back to `gpt-5.6-luna`.

**Every committed STT final used to become its own LLM turn, and a barge-in invalidated none of them.** So a caller who said "Hello?" into a stall was answered twice — once for the utterance that was already queued and once for the prod — and each reply made the next one later. `TurnChain.coalesce` (`transports/pipeline-turn-gate.ts`) is the fix: utterances arriving while a turn is in flight are merged and answered by exactly ONE further reply, and nothing the caller said is dropped on the way. Two properties are the whole point and are pinned as specs: the queued text is joined rather than discarded, and the queue does not strand work after `invalidateAll()` or after a terminate — the `callerTurnQueued` latch is cleared BEFORE the epoch check, or a turn invalidated while queued would wedge every later utterance in that session. A/B verified: defeating the coalescing fails exactly one spec. `createTurnDoors` moved here with it, which is what keeps `pipeline-transport.ts` under the 500-line source cap.

**`ASSEMBLYAI_LLM_DEFAULT_MODEL` is `gpt-5.6-luna`.** Four ids held it in one day, and this is the only one chosen on ANSWER QUALITY rather than on latency or catalog freshness. Matched per task against one tau2-bench retail baseline: luna **0.463** over 108 tasks, `gpt-5.6-sol` **0.19** over 16 sims, and `qwen3-next-80b-a3b` **0.212** over 33 matched tasks where luna scored **0.485** — 11 regressions against 2 improvements, McNemar two-sided p = 0.022. Time-to-first-token does not buy it back: qwen is ~2x faster to first token than luna (p50 664ms vs 832ms) and 3.4x faster than `gemini-3.7-flash` (2253ms), and fails more than twice as often. The failures are not latency-shaped — every regression was an authentication-by-name task, with the smaller models re-asking for a value they had already mis-heard instead of retrying a plausible confusion. A candidate default therefore needs a tau2 run, not a latency measurement.

**The id and `reasoningEffort` have to move TOGETHER**, which is why this is one change and not two. `TOOLS_REQUIRE_NO_REASONING` decides whether a bare `assemblyAILlm()` fills `reasoningEffort: "none"`, and the value has to be one the id ACCEPTS: `"none"` is REQUIRED on the `gpt-5.6` family, accepted with zero reasoning tokens on `qwen3-next-80b-a3b`, and REFUSED outright by every Gemini id, whose floor is `"low"` or `"minimal"`. A rejected effort is a 400 that the streaming path this SDK uses reports as a bare `500 {"message":"something went wrong"}` with the explanation stripped — so the wrong pairing type-checks, deploys, connects, and answers nothing. The constant's doc now carries the measured matrix per id, `define.test.ts` pins the id against the preset's effort, and `assemblyai.test.ts` states the invariant rather than the incidental fact: a bare factory must be tool-capable whichever side of the set the default sits on.

`aai:llm` retains epochs 5, 6 and 7, each with a frozen example pinning a distinct promise that survived the churn — the string shorthand, a bare factory that can still call tools, `"low"` still being expressible, and the descriptor shape.
